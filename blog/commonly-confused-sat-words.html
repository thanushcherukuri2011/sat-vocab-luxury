5 Commonly Confused SAT Vocabulary Words

Some SAT words don't just test whether you know a definition — they test whether you know it well enough to avoid mixing it up with a similar-looking or similar-sounding word. Here are five pairs that trip up even strong readers.

1. Complacent vs. Complaisant

Complacent means self-satisfied to the point of being unaware of danger — a smug kind of comfort.

Complaisant means eager to please, agreeable to others' wishes.

Memory trick: Complaisant sounds like "compliant" — both are about pleasing others.

2. Discreet vs. Discrete

Discreet means careful and tactful, avoiding unwanted attention.

Discrete means separate and distinct, individually distinguishable.

Memory trick: Discrete has two separate "e"s split by a "t" — separate letters for a word about separateness.

3. Ingenious vs. Ingenuous

Ingenious means cleverly inventive.

Ingenuous means innocent and unsuspecting, without guile.

Memory trick: Ingenious ends like "genius" — cleverness. Ingenuous is the odd one out, meaning the opposite of cunning.

4. Averse vs. Adverse

Averse describes a person's strong dislike or reluctance toward something (you are averse to something).

Adverse describes a condition that is unfavorable or harmful (adverse weather, adverse effects).

Memory trick: Averse is about a person's feelings; adverse is about a thing's effect.

5. Prescribe vs. Proscribe

Prescribe means to recommend or lay down as a rule — a doctor prescribes medicine.

Proscribe means to forbid, to officially ban something.

Memory trick: Proscribe sounds like "prohibit" — both start with a warning tone. Prescribe is the friendlier one.
