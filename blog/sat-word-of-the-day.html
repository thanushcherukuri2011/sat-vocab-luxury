SAT Word of the Day: Ephemeral

Every day we're picking one high-frequency SAT vocabulary word worth knowing cold before test day. Today's word: ephemeral.

Ephemeral
adjective

Meaning: Lasting for a very short time; fleeting.

Example: The cherry blossoms are famous partly because their beauty is so ephemeral — gone within a week or two of blooming.

Where it comes from

The word traces back to the Greek ephemeros, meaning "lasting only a day" — built from epi- ("on") and hemera ("day"). Originally, Greek physicians used it to describe a fever that broke within a single day. Over time, English borrowed the word and loosened its meaning to cover anything short-lived, not just illnesses.

Why it shows up on the SAT

Ephemeral is a favorite in reading passages about art, nature, and memory, since it captures the tension between beauty and impermanence — a common literary theme. It also pairs naturally with SAT-style "opposite meaning" questions, often contrasted against words like perpetual or enduring.

A memory trick

Think of a mayfly — an insect literally named for living just a single day as an adult. If "ephemeral" and "mayfly" both bring to mind something beautiful but gone almost as soon as it arrives, the word will stick.
